New DAFM-funded ADDRESS project to investigate antimicrobial resistance across One Health environments

The MicroBiomica Lab at University College Dublin is part of ADDRESS, a major research programme investigating how antimicrobial resistance emerges, evolves and spreads across interconnected agricultural, food-production and environmental systems.

ADDRESS stands for Antimicrobial Resistance Dynamics, Evolution and Emerging Threats in the Environment.

Maynooth University · UCD · Teagasc · University of Galway

Programme investment

€2.44m

awarded through the DAFM 2025 Thematic Research Call

The MicroBiomica contribution

At UCD, Dr Guerrino Macori and the MicroBiomica Lab will lead research combining long-read metagenomics, microbial genomics, bioinformatics and predictive modelling to investigate antimicrobial resistance reservoirs and transmission across One Health environments.

A major component of our work will be the generation and analysis of long-read metagenomic data from farm, food-production, environmental and wildlife sources. Using Oxford Nanopore sequencing alongside genomic and microbiological approaches, we will investigate microbial communities, antimicrobial resistance genes, mobile genetic elements and their genomic context.

An important research direction will be the study of microbial dark matter: microorganisms and genomic sequences that remain poorly represented in current reference databases. Rather than restricting AMR surveillance to already characterised organisms, we will investigate whether these uncharacterised components of microbial communities represent overlooked reservoirs of resistance genes and mobile genetic elements.

The project will also integrate metagenomic information with bacterial whole-genome sequencing, antimicrobial resistance phenotypes and environmental metadata. Computational approaches, including taxonomy-independent analysis, unsupervised learning and machine learning, will be developed and applied to identify patterns associated with the emergence, persistence and transmission of AMR.

Ultimately, this integrated information will contribute to predictive models and risk mapping aimed at identifying environments and conditions in which antimicrobial resistance may be more likely to emerge or spread.

Building research capacity in AMR and environmental genomics

ADDRESS represents a significant expansion of the MicroBiomica Lab's research at the intersection of antimicrobial resistance, environmental microbiology, long-read metagenomics, microbial dark matter and computational biology.

The programme will also support new research staff and PhD training at UCD, providing opportunities for researchers to work across experimental microbiology, sequencing, bioinformatics and data-driven approaches within a multidisciplinary One Health consortium.

The project forms part of DAFM's €37.5 million investment in 30 new research projects across Ireland's agri-food, forest and bioeconomy sectors.
